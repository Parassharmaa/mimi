# V17 strategy review: faithful on-policy multi-pair distillation

Date: 2026-07-26

Status: **literature review and diagnostic recommendation; no V17 training
contract exists**

## Decision

Do not run another same-data, same-objective fine-tune. First determine whether
the safe parent produces enough *real, on-policy* mistakes that are both:

1. worse than the licensed human reference under independent faithfulness
   evidence; and
2. assigned too much probability by the safe parent.

If that diagnostic passes, V17 should train on ranked candidate lists with a
faithfulness-first, confidence-aware CPO objective plus clean-reference and
safe-parent-retention losses. It should project conflict between the
preference/safety objective and the clean/retention objective—not merely
between two synthetic error objectives as V16 did.

In parallel, scope but do not yet train a single bidirectional 90–130M dense
encoder-decoder student with encoder-aware distillation. This is the most
credible untested capacity point under the 150 MB preference. A sparse MoE is a
later ablation only after a capacity-matched dense control and native MLX
profiling justify its routing and residency complexity.

## What V16 established

V16 proved that symmetric PCGrad can remove conflict between the omission and
repetition objectives: 46 of 50 steps had a negative pre-projection cosine and
all projected cosines were positive. It did **not** establish that this was the
important conflict. The selected step still failed the V12 omission regression
barrier and the active-risk improvement gates.

The missing measurement was the gradient relationship between:

- the combined safety/preference objective; and
- clean licensed-reference MLE plus retention to the distributable safe
  parent.

V16 also relied on constructed deletion/repetition negatives. Those are useful
for controlled diagnosis, but they do not show that the parent naturally
visits the same error distribution at decoding time.

## Literature review

### Confidence and reward should select the hard examples

CRPO selects preference pairs using both reward gap and the policy's own
confidence. The important case is not merely a bad candidate; it is a bad
candidate the current policy prefers or finds deceptively plausible. The paper
also reports that this idea transfers from decoder-only LLMs to an
encoder-decoder NLLB model:
[CRPO: Confidence-Reward Driven Preference Optimization for Machine
Translation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.31/).

This is a closer match to Mimi than another uniform corpus continuation.
Mimi's compact parent already assigns high teacher-forced preference to many
constructed correct targets, while free-running failures remain. V17 should
therefore mine the parent's actual rollouts and select failures using
length-normalized sequence confidence.

### Partial omissions require faithfulness-aware, multi-pair supervision

M²PO reports a systematic blind spot in QE metrics for fluent partial
hallucinations and omissions. It combines a general QE signal with a discrete
semantic-alignment signal, then gradually incorporates model confidence. It
also retains the whole ranked candidate list instead of discarding
intermediate errors in a best-versus-worst reduction:
[M²PO: Multi-Perspective Multi-Pair Preference Optimization for Machine
Translation](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.469/).

The exact paper recipe is not directly portable: its experiments use 4B and 9B
decoder-only models, a large QE model, and an LLM semantic-alignment
classifier. Its causal lesson is still highly relevant. Mimi should represent
the reference, strong alternatives, partial errors, and severe errors as an
ordered list. Omission and critical-token preservation must be an explicit
faithfulness component, not something inferred from a single scalar COMET
score.

Preference training on ambiguous terminology similarly found that combining
SFT with both term-specific and full-sequence preference objectives improved
term accuracy without a significant COMET loss:
[Learning to Translate Ambiguous Terminology by Preference Optimization on
Post-Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03580). That supports keeping a
reference-anchored generation loss while adding narrow terminology and
faithfulness comparisons.

### The reward used for selection cannot certify the result

An empirical CPO study found gains on the optimized alignment metric but
instability between neural and lexical downstream metrics. It also found that
candidate lists generated by the base model can be competitive with lists from
multiple systems:
[Is Preference Alignment Always the Best Option to Enhance LLM-Based
Translation?](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wmt-1.127/).

Minimum-Bayes-risk work independently shows that a neural utility metric can
overestimate outputs selected with that metric, and that an ensemble reduces
but does not eliminate this bias:
[Mitigating Metric Bias in Minimum Bayes Risk
Decoding](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wmt-1.109/). Direct metric
optimization has produced universal adversarial translations for learned
metrics in other MT settings:
[BLEURT Has Universal Translations](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.297/).

COMET is therefore one input to candidate mining, never the V17 success
criterion by itself. Reference-overlap metrics remain useful because they are
sensitive to entities and numbers that neural metrics can miss:
[BLEU Meets COMET](https://aclanthology.org/2023.eamt-1.6/).

### Distillation should transfer encoder knowledge

Sequence-level distillation primarily supervises the decoder. A 2025
low-resource NMT study adds cosine alignment between mean-pooled teacher and
student encoder states and reports better out-of-domain generalization than
vanilla sequence distillation:
[Encoder-Aware Sequence-Level Knowledge Distillation for Low-Resource
NMT](https://aclanthology.org/2025.loresmt-1.15/).

The paper's small experiment is not evidence that its exact loss weight or
randomly initialized T5 architecture will work for English↔Japanese. It does
identify a missing signal in Mimi's prior sequence-distillation arms. A future
larger student should align encoder representations from an open teacher while
also learning licensed human references and filtered final-output-only teacher
translations. No teacher chain-of-thought or reasoning trace is needed or
retained.

### Small MoE is promising, but not yet Mimi's next move

CoSMoEs provides current evidence that sparse experts can outperform dense
models in fair active-parameter comparisons and proposes low-rank,
weight-decomposed experts plus block-wise expert selection:
[CoSMoEs: Compact Sparse Mixture of
Experts](https://aclanthology.org/2026.alvr-main.4/). Its evaluated models are
roughly 1B–3B language models with multi-gigabyte peak memory, not sub-500 MB
translation models. The paper also measures slower GPU generation for
resident MoE variants and a latency penalty when experts are offloaded.

THOR-MoE shows that translation routing benefits from task and global context
rather than only local token state:
[THOR-MoE](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1040/). That makes a
production-quality new router more—not less—complex.

Mimi has already rejected routed combinations whose experts share the same
failures. The next causal question is whether modest dense capacity plus better
distillation repairs fidelity. MoE becomes justified only if a dense
capacity-matched student passes quality but misses a measured compute target,
or if independently useful experts demonstrate complementary held-out wins.

## Independent consultation

An authenticated, no-tools Claude CLI consultation used `claude-sonnet-5` with
high effort and no session persistence. It was an architecture critique, not a
human review, training label, or evaluation result. Its strongest actionable
criticism agrees with the evidence above: V16 projected omission against
repetition, but did not measure safety/preferences against clean MLE and
retention. It also recommended a larger minimum hard-pair count, confidence
intervals, a selection-precision audit, and a modest dense encoder-aware
capacity probe before MoE.

Because the project is reviewer-free, any semantic precision audit will use
exact independent consensus from Claude Sonnet 5 and Claude Opus 5, with
strict structured final answers and no reasoning retention. These models must
not also be the sole final evaluator of a trained V17 candidate.

## V17 diagnostic design

The diagnostic is training-free and must be reproducible before any V17
contract is frozen.

### 1. Authenticated sources

- Use only source/reference pairs with distributable training rights and
  row-level provenance.
- Positive targets are licensed human references or already admitted,
  final-output-only synthetic translations with their existing eligibility
  labels preserved.
- Exclude every public validation, V12–V16 regression, sealed promotion, and
  protected source.
- Generated rollout text is negative or ranking evidence only. It never
  becomes an unreviewed positive target.

### 2. On-policy candidate lists

For each source and direction, generate a frozen list from the safe parent:

- one greedy candidate;
- deterministic beam alternatives where available;
- multiple seeded stochastic candidates at preregistered temperatures and
  top-p values;
- the licensed reference as the anchor.

Record token IDs, stop reason, length-normalized log probability, decoding
configuration, model hash, and source/reference provenance. Deduplicate exact
candidate text before scoring. Long sources must be represented explicitly;
they may not be reduced to short-sentence-only mining.

### 3. Faithfulness-first selection

Score candidate lists with separate components:

- pinned reference-aware COMET;
- chrF++ and sacreBLEU against the licensed reference;
- deterministic preservation of numbers, identifiers, URLs, placeholders,
  markup, code spans, and generation-stop structure;
- explicit omission, addition, negation, named-entity, terminology, register,
  and repetition labels from exact Sonnet-5/Opus-5 consensus on the
  preselected hard set.

Do not collapse these into one opaque success metric. The selection score may
rank candidates, but admission requires:

- both independent judges prefer the reference or a verified stronger
  candidate to the rejected rollout;
- neither judge reports a critical error in the chosen candidate;
- at least one judge-supported error label explains the rejection; and
- the safe parent assigns the rejected rollout an equal or higher
  length-normalized probability, or the pair falls inside a frozen
  near-margin band.

Keep the entire verified ranking where possible. Pairwise examples should be
derived deterministically from that list, including intermediate partial
errors rather than only the easiest best-versus-worst contrast.

### 4. Gradient audit

On fixed, disjoint batches, compute full-gradient norms and pairwise cosines
for:

- multi-pair CPO plus chosen-sequence NLL;
- critical-faithfulness sequence ranking;
- clean licensed-reference MLE;
- KL/logit retention to the safe parent.

The primary audit is combined preference/safety versus combined MLE/retention.
Also compare the unprojected joint gradient with PCGrad applied at that
boundary. No optimizer is selected from translation results.

### 5. Diagnostic go/no-go

The later immutable diagnostic contract should freeze the exact thresholds.
The literature review recommends no training unless all of these are met:

- at least 1,500 independently verified hard comparisons in total, with at
  least 600 per direction;
- at least 15% representation for each of omission, repetition, and
  Japanese-sensitive negation/particle/register/terminology risk, allowing
  documented overlap;
- at least 85% pair-label precision in a deterministic stratified
  Sonnet-5/Opus-5 re-audit, with a paired bootstrap lower bound reported;
- at least 50% negative cosine incidence between preference/safety and
  MLE/retention across fixed batches;
- median preference/safety-to-MLE/retention gradient-norm ratio of at least
  0.10;
- finite gradients, nontrivial margins, both directions, and explicit long
  source coverage; and
- byte-identical reproduction of the data, ranking, and diagnostic manifests
  from pinned inputs.

If the hard set is too small, label precision is weak, or gradients do not
conflict, V17 is a no-go. Do not relax the thresholds or manufacture more
synthetic corruptions after seeing the result.

## Conditional V17 training shape

Only a passed diagnostic can authorize a separate immutable training contract.
The likely single-arm shape is:

- safe-parent initialization and retention teacher;
- reference-free CPO formulation to avoid a second resident model;
- margin-weighted multi-pair comparisons from verified on-policy lists;
- chosen-sequence NLL and clean licensed-reference replay;
- PCGrad only across the empirically conflicting
  preference/safety-versus-MLE/retention boundary;
- fixed small learning rate, fixed steps, and fixed checkpoints;
- no architecture, tokenizer, quantization, decoder, bundle, or app change.

Selection must begin with fresh source-disjoint corpus, long-document,
critical-meaning, negation, terminology, and historical V12–V16 barriers.
COMET, chrF++, BLEU, deterministic critical checks, and independent blinded
semantic judges all remain required. A metric used to rank training candidates
cannot be the sole promotion signal.

## Conditional 90–130M dense student

V17 and a new capacity probe answer different questions. The capacity probe
should be scoped now and trained only under its own contract:

- one bidirectional encoder-decoder with explicit direction tags;
- shared English/Japanese SentencePiece vocabulary;
- approximately 90–130M parameters before quantization;
- deep encoder and shallow decoder, with exact alternatives fixed by an
  architecture-only compute/size analysis;
- initialization from a compact pretrained multilingual model where licenses
  and tensor compatibility permit, rather than training from scratch;
- sequence-level final-output distillation from stronger open teachers;
- encoder-state alignment through a training-only projection;
- licensed human-reference replay and safe-parent distillation;
- one 4-bit MLX pack targeted below 150,000,000 bytes.

At 4-bit weight storage, this parameter range leaves practical room for
quantization metadata, tokenizer assets, manifests, and notices inside the
150 MB model target. That estimate is not a bundle claim; the exact pack,
resident memory, warm p50/p95, long-input behavior, and Swift/Python parity
must still be measured.

## Stop rules

- No V17 training before the diagnostic and immutable contract.
- No protected or sealed evaluation before preregistered pre-semantic gates.
- No q4 conversion, COMET promotion run, runtime comparison, bundle change,
  app integration, release, or Hugging Face upload for a failed checkpoint.
- No Apple model dependency in the intended final backend.
- The existing bundled/local development translator remains unchanged until a
  candidate clears every quality, safety, license, size, memory, latency, and
  parity gate.
